Product Design · Hack Week

Digital HQ

Zurich Insurance Australia needed a simple solution to the all too common problem of internal communication. As part of a hack week, I designed a Microsoft Teams app to provide a single, at-a-glance overview of the business, that ended up being built and shipped to the entire company.

Role
UX / UI Designer
Client
Zurich Insurance Australia
Format
One-week hack week
Platform
Microsoft Teams app

01 — The brief

Too many channels, not enough clarity

The hack week brief started with a "how might we" question: how could Zurich Australia create a more integrated, efficient way for people to communicate internally? I joined as the UX designer on a diverse team pulled together from across the business.

We spent the first couple of days just mapping how communication actually worked. We explored everything from Teams channels, to Outlook inboxes, to Workplace posts, the intranet, and even the public website. We quickly discovered there was lots of overlap, lots of duplication, and no single place that told you what mattered today.

Doing this helped us narrow down a clear problem statement we could design against: employees were doing a daily scavenger hunt every morning just to feel caught up.

02 — Research

A week of listening first

Hack weeks move fast, but to ensure what we built actually met the needs of our users and the organisation, we needed our decisions to be grounded in research. As the team's user experience expert, I led a UX research initiative that saw me running internal interviews with Zurich employees to understand their morning routines and where communication broke down. I also spoke to people at other companies who'd tackled similar problems, including someone at Salesforce who described how a consolidated comms view had simplified things on their side. That external conversation was useful. It didn't give us a template to copy, but it confirmed we weren't chasing a niche problem.

Beyond that, the research would also involve the following:

Discovery workshops

Kick-off sessions with the hack week team to explore the problem statement and generate early ideas together.

User interviews

One-on-ones with employees across the business about how they communicate day to day, and what slows them down.

Empathy mapping

I ran the team through an empathy mapping exercise so we had a shared picture of who we were designing for.

Card sorting

We grouped findings from interviews and workshops into themes, helping to cut through the noise and identify key areas for us to work on.

Personas

I created a series of user personas based on what we'd heard. These helped show a clear split in our target audience, where some wanted everything in one place; while others were wary of yet another channel.

User journeys

Journey maps for the current morning routine, and sketches of how a centralised view might change it.

03 — Design

Meet people where they already are

One thing kept coming up in interviews: most people opened Teams first thing in the morning. Before even Outlook or the intranet. Clearly there could be an opportunity here.

With our research in hand, we undertook ideation workshops to generate and decide on potential solutions. As the designer I then took on the role of sketching the leading solutions to help the team visualise them. This ultimately led to our concept: a "Digital HQ" that lived inside Teams and pulled all the important stuff into one screen. The stuff in question would be news, your calendar, quick links to the tools you use constantly, feeds from the channels you care about.

The catch was that it had to be worth opening. A clear theme from our research was that if the view wasn't genuinely useful, it would just become another tab people ignored, so it was critical to consider this as I proceeded to the wire-framing stage.

Getting the structure right before the polish

To create the mockups for the Digital HQ, I considered what employees had told us they typically checked first thing on a given morning: a personalised header, app shortcuts, a banner area for company announcements, quick links, a "My Day" schedule panel, news, and configurable feeds at the bottom.

I brought these back to the team and kept iterating based on feedback from different parts of the business, including Comms who had opinions about the banner, and IT cared about which quick links made the cut. This feedback was both expected and useful, and helped me to shape the designs to ensure they were meeting needs from across the business.

Once the structure felt solid, I built prototypes and ran testing sessions with employees matched to the personas we'd developed. People reacted well to having their schedule and news on the same screen. The feeds section needed the most refinement (too many columns felt overwhelming, too few felt incomplete), and thus we landed on the three customisable feeds with scrollable content.

A morning view that pulls everything together

The finished design uses Zurich's brand language and sits natively inside Teams as a pinned app. It provides each employee with a customised view, showing their photo, their schedule and their feeds.

The banner carousel then handles big company announcements, while quick links get people to People Portal, IT self-service, policies, and the other tools they use regularly (but often lose the links to!). Breaking news opens in a modal so you can read (or watch) without leaving the page.

04 — Outcome

From hack week sketch to company-wide rollout

Our project was selected for further funding after hack week, which doesn't happen to every team, and I think highlights how my team successfully took a user needs driven approach, that offered a real solution to genuine problems faced by everyday Zurich employees.

The designs I produced during the week became the foundation for what was built and shipped, and Digital HQ is now the landing page on Microsoft Teams for every Zurich employee in Australia.

Looking back, the thing that made it work was picking one entry point (Teams) and designing something people would actually want to open before their first meeting. The research-heavy start of the week earned us the right to move fast on design later in the week.

Selected for funding

The hack week team’s project was chosen for further development out of all the week’s submissions.

Shipped to all of Australia

Digital HQ went from week-one wireframes to the default Teams landing page for Zurich Australia employees.

One morning routine

News, schedule, quick links, and team feeds in a single view. Replacing the daily check across multiple channels.

Efficient research

Personas, journeys, interviews, and card sorting in five days. With enough rigour to design with confidence, but not too much to slow the team down.
